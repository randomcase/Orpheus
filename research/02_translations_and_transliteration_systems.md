# Multi-System Transliterations, Phonetics, & Translations Across 2,600 Years

## 1. Why Multiple Transliteration Systems Matter

Orpheus's primary sources span **seven centuries of Greek** (Archaic Doric, Classical Attic, Hellenistic Koine, Patristic/Byzantine Greek) and **thirteen centuries of Latin** (Augustan Classical Latin to Medieval Catholic Scholastic Latin). No single spelling system captures how these texts sounded to different communities:

| System | Primary Audience | Key Features | Historical Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ISO 843 Academic** | Classics, epigraphy, & philology | Reversible letter-for-letter mapping; macrons (`ē`, `ō`); `h` for rough breathing | Exact textual reconstruction back to Greek script |
| **ALA-LC Romanization** | Library catalogs & humanities indexing | `ch`, `ph`, `th`, `ps`; preserves classical diphthongs (`eu`, `ou`) | Standardized library citation across archives |
| **5th-c. BCE Attic IPA** | Historical linguistics & reconstructed performance | True aspirated stops (`pʰ`, `tʰ`, `kʰ`), long vowels, tonal pitch contour | Captures how Euripides' and Plato's Athens heard Orpheus |
| **Byzantine / Liturgical Greek** | Patristic scholars & Eastern chant | Iotacism (`η/ι/υ/ει/οι` -> `/i/`), fricatives (`β` -> `v`, `δ` -> `dh`, `γ` -> `gh`) | Captures how Clement of Alexandria and early Christians chanted Greek |
| **Classical vs. Ecclesiastical Latin** | Roman poetry vs. Catholic liturgy | Classical `v=[w]`, `c=[k]`, `ae=[ae̯]` vs. Ecclesiastical `v=[v]`, `c/ce=[tʃ]`, `ae=[ɛ]` | Reveals the acoustic shift from Virgil (`29 BCE`) to Boethius (`524 CE`) and medieval hymns |

## 2. Case Studies in Translation Shift

### A. From *Dementia* (Virgil) to *Avidus Videndi* (Ovid)
* **Virgil (*Georgics* IV.488–491, 29 BCE):** Uses *subita incautum dementia cepit amantem* ("sudden madness seized the heedless lover") and ends the clause brutally on *immemor heu! victusque animi respexit*. In Augustan Rome recovering from civil war, Orpheus's failure is framed as an irrational civic breakdown contrasted with Aristaeus's disciplined ritual renewal.
* **Ovid (*Metamorphoses* X.56–57, 8 CE):** Rewrites the motive entirely: *ne deficeret, metuens avidusque videndi / flexit amans oculos* ("fearing lest she faint, and eager to look upon her, the lover turned his eyes"). Twelve years into stable Augustan peace, Ovid shifts tragedy from divine madness into tender, anxious human vulnerability.

### B. Boethius's Reversal of *Respicit* (524 CE)
In Virgil and Ovid, **Orpheus** is the subject of *respexit* ("he looked back [at Eurydice]"). Five centuries later in *Consolation of Philosophy* III.m12, **Boethius** makes the story itself the subject: *Vos haec fabula respicit* ("This fable looks upon *you*"). The translator must preserve that mirror-flip—the reader, not Orpheus alone, stands on trial at the edge of light.
